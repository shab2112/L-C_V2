# Lockwood & Carter — Digital Brand & Website Design Direction

**Version 3.1 · August 2026**  
**Status:** Design direction for website redesign, social templates, campaigns, and implementation handoff

---

## 1. The brief

Lockwood & Carter is a Dubai-based premium real estate advisory serving buyers and investors from the UAE and around the world. Its identity should combine contemporary Dubai property expertise with the restraint, craft, and editorial confidence associated with modern British advisory brands. This is a **British-inspired** design language, not a claim of British origin, longevity, or institutional heritage.

This document is the design source of truth for the website and its supporting digital assets. It translates the brand ambition into decisions a designer or developer can execute.

### Positioning

> **British-inspired refinement. Dubai property expertise. Global perspective.**

The brand is not selling generic luxury. It is selling confident access to Dubai: credible local guidance, carefully selected opportunities, strong developer relationships, and a simpler buying experience for international clients.

### Desired impression

Within the first 10 seconds, a visitor should understand:

1. Lockwood & Carter is a credible Dubai brokerage.
2. It serves international as well as UAE-based buyers.
3. It offers experienced human advice, supported by useful technology.
4. It represents a considered selection of properties—not an undifferentiated portal feed.

### Brand personality

**Considered · Assured · Discerning · Cultured · Human**

The tone is contemporary and confident, never loud. Heritage is communicated through typographic craft, proportion, materiality, and service—not borrowed aristocratic symbols or invented history.

### Brand idea

**Contemporary British Advisory**

This idea governs every brand choice. “British” means measured language, discretion, fine editorial typography, tailored detail, and respect for craft. “Contemporary” prevents the identity from becoming nostalgic or ceremonial. “Advisory” keeps people, judgment, and service ahead of property inventory or technology.

### Creative tension

| British-inspired character | Dubai expression |
|---|---|
| Parchment, ink, oxblood, antique brass | Limestone, water, warm light, glass |
| Editorial authority and restraint | Cinematic scale and international energy |
| Tailoring, fine rules, crafted details | Contemporary architecture and ambitious living |
| Understatement and considered language | Clear opportunity and confident action |

Neither side should dominate. Without the first, the identity becomes generic Dubai luxury; without the second, it becomes an imitation London heritage brand.

## 2. Review of the previous direction

### Keep

- The diagnosis that the ornate cartouche, metallic bevels, script, and multiple type styles make the current identity feel dated.
- The deep-navy base. It is distinctive, credible, and suitable for cinematic Dubai imagery.
- A restrained antique-brass accent rather than bright or heavily rendered gold.
- Full-bleed property imagery, editorial typography, and generous space.
- The idea of presenting market intelligence through calm, sourced data modules.
- A simplified L&C monogram as the long-term small-format mark.

### Correct or refine

- **Brokerage first; technology second.** DARIE can support discovery, but it should not become the master brand or dominate every surface. International clients ultimately need confidence in the advisors, process, and properties.
- **Avoid unverified superlatives.** Claims such as “world’s first,” “3× faster,” or partnership totals should only appear when documented, current, and legally approved.
- **Do not imitate SERHANT. or Bogard.** Borrow principles—media fluency, decisive typography, strong property storytelling, and visible human expertise—not their exact visual language.
- **The serif choice must be fixed.** A list of possible fonts is not a design system. This version chooses a primary pairing.
- **Pill buttons are not the default.** They can make the site feel like a SaaS template. The brand should use architectural, low-radius controls.
- **The system must include UX, responsiveness, accessibility, motion, page hierarchy, and conversion rules.** Visual taste alone will not produce a premium website.
- **Dubai should be present without cliché.** Avoid repetitive Burj Khalifa hero shots, gold skylines, flags, and “city of the future” language. Show specific places, homes, views, and lived experience.

## 3. Benchmark principles

The relevant lesson from leading US luxury brokerages is not “use black, white, and a large logo.” It is the combination of property, personality, media, and proof.

| Benchmark principle | Lockwood & Carter interpretation |
|---|---|
| Properties are treated as brands | Give flagship listings an editorial story, film, location context, and investment case—not only a specification grid. |
| Advisors are visible | Show named people, expertise, languages, and direct routes to consultation. Avoid hiding the company behind AI. |
| Media creates reach | Build the site around cinematic tours, neighbourhood stories, market briefings, and short-form content. |
| Proof is specific | Use verifiable transactions, client stories, developer relationships, RERA details, and sourced market data. |
| Design is decisive | Use a limited palette, one typography system, strong composition, and fewer—but better—messages per screen. |

## 4. Brand architecture and messaging hierarchy

### Master brand

**Lockwood & Carter** is the primary identity on every page and campaign.

### Core proposition

**Leading property advisors, powered by smart tools.**

Use this as a supporting line, not necessarily the main homepage headline. The hero should state a visitor benefit more directly.

### Recommended homepage message

**Eyebrow:** DUBAI PROPERTY ADVISORY  
**Headline:** Find your place in Dubai.  
**Support:** Local expertise, carefully selected properties, and clear guidance for buyers and investors worldwide.  
**Primary CTA:** Explore Properties  
**Secondary CTA:** Consult an Advisor

Alternative campaign line:

> **Dubai, considered.**

Use it selectively for reports, films, and high-level brand campaigns—not as a replacement for clear conversion copy.

Approved supporting lines:

- **Considered advice. Exceptional Dubai property.**
- **A considered perspective on Dubai property.**
- **Local knowledge. Global perspective.**

Do not use “British heritage,” “British roots,” “London-founded,” or similar provenance language unless it is factually documented. “British-inspired” is an internal creative description, not a headline that needs to appear repeatedly in customer copy.

### Brand pillars

| Pillar | What it means | Evidence to show |
|---|---|---|
| Local intelligence | Practical, current knowledge of Dubai communities and developments | Area guides, advisor commentary, market reports, developer access |
| Global service | A buying journey designed for overseas clients | Languages, remote viewing, transaction steps, time-zone-friendly consultation |
| Curated opportunity | Selection and explanation instead of overwhelming inventory | Editor’s picks, investment rationale, comparison tools, project due diligence |
| Human advice + smart tools | Technology improves speed and clarity while advisors remain accountable | DARIE discovery, saved shortlist, advisor review, transparent next steps |

### Voice

- Clear, informed, composed, and direct.
- Premium through precision, understatement, and judgment—not exaggerated adjectives.
- Explain Dubai-specific processes in plain English.
- Prefer “selected,” “well-positioned,” and specific proof over “exclusive,” “iconic,” “unparalleled,” or “ultimate.”
- Never imply guaranteed returns, appreciation, residency eligibility, or completion outcomes.

### British-inspired writing cues

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| “A well-positioned waterfront residence” | “An unparalleled luxury masterpiece” |
| “Our view” or “What to consider” | “You cannot miss this opportunity” |
| “Speak with an advisor” | “Unlock your dream lifestyle now” |
| “Selected for location, design, and long-term relevance” | “Handpicked elite investment with guaranteed growth” |
| Calm, complete sentences | Hype, excessive exclamation marks, and stacked superlatives |

Use British English consistently in brand and editorial copy: `neighbourhood`, `programme`, `personalised`, and `centre`. Preserve official development names and quoted source terminology.

## 5. Visual concept: Contemporary British Advisory

The visual world combines four ideas:

1. **British editorial authority** — Baskerville-influenced typography, measured hierarchy, literary pacing, and confident whitespace.
2. **Tailored precision** — fine rules, disciplined grids, aligned details, and a sense that every element has been fitted rather than decorated.
3. **Modern heritage** — parchment, ink, oxblood, and antique brass used sparingly, without crests or theatrical nostalgia.
4. **Dubai atmosphere** — light, water, limestone, glass, desert warmth, and contemporary urban living.

The result should feel like a respected British property journal reinterpreted for contemporary Dubai: composed, worldly, and quietly distinctive—not a dark “luxury template,” country-club pastiche, or technology dashboard.

### Signature composition

- Large photographic or film-led opening.
- Restrained typographic overlay with one clear action.
- Alternation between cinematic dark sections and warm editorial light sections.
- Occasional asymmetric image crops to create pace.
- Narrow data and accreditation bands that add evidence without clutter.
- Small moments of tailored detail: double rules, folio numbers, editorial captions, monogram stamps, and oxblood highlights.

### Avoid

- Crowns, lions, shields, coats of arms, heraldic foliage, wax-seal effects, faux family crests, and invented Latin mottos.
- Baroque flourishes, metallic effects, distressed paper, and marble UI textures.
- Generic black-and-gold luxury styling.
- Literal Union Jack colours, London landmarks, red telephone boxes, equestrian clichés, and costume-drama styling.
- Excessive glassmorphism, floating cards, particles, circuit lines, or glowing AI orbs.
- Full-screen video without legible controls, performance fallbacks, or a reduced-motion alternative.
- Copying a reference site section-for-section.

## 6. Logo system

### Approved final logo

![Lockwood & Carter Real Estate — approved final logo](../deliverables/lockwood-carter-final-logo-transparent.png)

The artwork above is the approved master logo for Lockwood & Carter Real Estate. It combines the ornate midnight-navy frame and integrated **L&C** monogram with an antique-gold ampersand, the established `Lockwood & Carter` wordmark, the `REAL ESTATE` descriptor, and the lower ornamental flourish. The transparent PNG must be used as the reference raster master; its lettering, monogram construction, colour boundaries, ornament, spacing, and proportions must not be regenerated or informally redrawn.

### Brand-mark principle

The logo expresses established character through customised lettering, the integrated monogram, and ornamental craft. It should appear consistently across the website, Dubai sales-gallery applications, property films, reports, and formal collateral without changing construction.

### Primary treatment

- Use the approved full logo at a size where its fine details remain legible.
- Do not add animation, bevel, glow, shadow, or extra gold effects.
- Use the approved monogram-only asset for compact placements where the full lockup becomes illegible.
- Do not redraw, typeset, trace, or regenerate the logo informally in Canva or an image-generation tool.
- Preserve the exact antique-gold ampersand, including the narrow gold stroke passing behind the navy **C**.

### Artwork and future production files

- Produce a professionally traced vector master from this approved artwork without changing its letterforms, flourishes, overlaps, colour boundaries, spacing, or proportions.
- Maintain full-lockup, wordmark-only, and monogram-only files derived from the same master construction.
- Supply SVG, PDF, EPS, and transparent PNG exports in the approved navy-and-antique-gold treatment, plus one-colour navy, white, and black variants.
- Metallic foil may be used as a physical production finish, but the digital master must remain flat and must not use bevels, glitter, glow, or simulated metallic reflections.
- Test each approved export at favicon size, mobile-navigation size, social-avatar size, signage, and watermark scale. Use the monogram rather than simplifying the full logo ad hoc.

### Required logo variants

| Variant | Construction | Primary use |
|---|---|---|
| Full approved lockup | Monogram + `Lockwood & Carter` + `REAL ESTATE` + lower flourish | Reports, formal stationery, signage, campaign end frames |
| Wordmark-only | `Lockwood & Carter` + `REAL ESTATE` | Website header and horizontal placements |
| L&C monogram | Approved framed monogram with gold ampersand | Favicon, avatar, watermark, social profile, compact brand stamp |
| One-colour lockup | Exact approved artwork in a single colour | Embossing, engraving, stamps, restricted-colour production |

### Logo colour rules

- Default light-background mark: Midnight Navy on Parchment or white.
- Default dark-background mark: Parchment or white on Midnight Navy.
- Antique Brass is used for the ampersand in the approved two-colour logo and may also be used for controlled print finishes.
- Oxblood may appear beside the logo as a rule or field; the full mark should not default to oxblood.
- The logo must always have a one-colour version. Never depend on foil, embossing, texture, or animation for recognition.

### Clear-space and misuse

- Minimum clear space: the cap height of the **L** around all sides.
- Minimum digital size: full lockup 240 px wide; wordmark-only 160 px wide; monogram 48 px. Use larger sizes whenever the ornamental detail must remain clearly visible.
- Never stretch, outline, recolour outside approved variants, place on a busy image without a solid field, or combine old and new marks in one asset.

## 7. Colour system

The system is ink-and-parchment led. Midnight navy provides continuity; oxblood and antique brass introduce a restrained British editorial signature; Dubai limestone and warm light prevent the identity from becoming sombre.

| Token | Hex | Primary use |
|---|---:|---|
| `ink-950` | `#101820` | Deep footer, editorial ink, cinema overlays |
| `midnight-900` | `#122238` | Primary brand field and navigation |
| `midnight-800` | `#1D334E` | Raised dark surfaces |
| `parchment-050` | `#F5F0E6` | Primary light background |
| `limestone-100` | `#E6DED0` | Secondary fields, rules, Dubai material cue |
| `charcoal-800` | `#292B2D` | Body text on light |
| `white` | `#FFFFFF` | High-contrast text and UI |
| `brass-500` | `#B49A68` | Fine rules, large display detail, print finish reference |
| `brass-700` | `#6F5A35` | Accessible accent text on light |
| `oxblood-700` | `#6D2636` | Editorial accent, active state, campaign punctuation |
| `oxblood-800` | `#531D2A` | Accessible oxblood fields and text where verified |
| `signal-blue` | `#70A7D8` | DARIE listening/active state only |
| `success` | `#35735A` | Positive functional state |
| `error` | `#A84C46` | Error and validation state |

### Usage ratio

- 58% parchment/limestone/white
- 32% midnight/ink/charcoal
- 7% oxblood/brass
- 3% functional colours

This ratio creates a more international editorial feeling than an almost entirely dark website.

### Rules

- Brass is a detail colour, not a default text colour or button fill.
- Oxblood should appear once or twice per viewport at most: an active rule, editorial label, report cover, or highly considered CTA state.
- Use `brass-700`, midnight, oxblood-800, or charcoal for text on parchment only after contrast verification.
- Use white for body copy on dark backgrounds when muted text fails contrast.
- Verify every foreground/background pair to WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and interface boundaries.
- Status colours must always be paired with text or an icon; colour cannot carry meaning alone.

## 8. Typography

### Primary pairing

| Role | Typeface | Weights | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | **Libre Baskerville** | 400, 400 italic, 700 | Hero statements, section titles, editorial quotes, report titles |
| UI and body | **Manrope** | 400, 500, 600 | Navigation, body copy, filters, labels, buttons, data |
| Fallback | Georgia / Arial / system sans | — | Loading and unavailable-font states |

Libre Baskerville gives the system a credible British typographic reference while remaining open and practical for digital use. Manrope keeps interfaces contemporary and globally legible. For a future premium identity commission, a licensed Baskerville revival or custom wordmark may replace Libre Baskerville in display applications, but the proportions and hierarchy must remain stable. Do not add Cormorant, Playfair Display, Montserrat, Inter, or script fonts to routine brand work.

### Responsive scale

| Style | Desktop | Mobile | Line height |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Display XL | 80–104 px | 46–58 px | 0.96–1.02 |
| Display L | 56–72 px | 38–46 px | 1.02–1.08 |
| H2 | 38–48 px | 30–36 px | 1.08–1.15 |
| H3 | 24–30 px | 22–26 px | 1.15–1.25 |
| Body L | 18–20 px | 17–18 px | 1.55–1.65 |
| Body | 16 px | 16 px | 1.55–1.7 |
| Label | 12–13 px | 12 px | 1.3 |

### Editorial type details

- Large serif headlines use sentence case, not constant title case.
- Use the Baskerville italic only for short, human emphasis: a location, quotation, or editorial kicker—not entire paragraphs.
- Use small-cap-style Manrope labels with `0.14em` tracking for descriptors, folios, dates, and section markers.
- Use hanging punctuation and carefully balanced line breaks on campaign headlines.
- Prices and investment data remain in Manrope with tabular numerals; do not set functional numbers in the serif.

### Rules

- Body line length: 55–72 characters.
- Eyebrows: uppercase Manrope 600, 0.12em tracking; never exceed one line.
- Do not letter-space serif headlines.
- Use tabular numerals for prices, yields, dates, and comparison tables.
- Avoid italics for long copy and all-caps paragraphs.
- Arabic content requires a tested companion typeface and right-to-left layouts; do not rely on browser substitution. Recommended starting point: **Noto Sans Arabic** for UI, tested alongside Manrope.

## 9. Grid, spacing, and shape

### Layout

- Maximum content width: 1440 px.
- Desktop: 12 columns, 24 px gutters, 64–96 px outer margin.
- Tablet: 8 columns, 24 px gutters, 32 px outer margin.
- Mobile: 4 columns, 16 px gutters, 20 px outer margin.
- Section spacing: 128–176 px desktop; 72–96 px mobile.
- Use an 8 px spacing base with 4 px permitted for fine optical adjustment.

### Shape language

- Cards and media: 0–4 px radius.
- Buttons and inputs: 2 px radius.
- Chips and status labels may use a full pill only when their compact metadata function is clear.
- Borders: 1 px, low contrast.
- Shadows: rare and soft; rely on spacing, tone, and rules for hierarchy.
- Signature detail: a paired 1 px/1 px rule separated by 3 px may appear on report covers, formal CTAs, and section openings. Do not use it around every card.
- Monogram stamps may sit in 40–56 px square or oval fields; never use a faux wax-seal texture.

### Editorial layout signatures

- Use folio-style section numbers such as `01 — RESIDENCES` on long editorial pages.
- Allow one asymmetrical image/typography composition per page section group; maintain alignment elsewhere.
- Captions sit below imagery in 12–13 px Manrope with location, status, and image qualification where needed.
- Pull quotes may use a 1 px oxblood rule and Baskerville italic, but must remain factual and attributable.

### Buttons

| Variant | Treatment | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Navy field / white text; white field / navy text on dark | One main action per section |
| Secondary | Transparent, 1 px current-colour border | Alternative action |
| Text link | Text + restrained arrow movement | Editorial journeys |
| DARIE action | Navy field with signal-blue state | Voice interaction only |

Minimum height 48 px; minimum touch target 44 × 44 px. Use action labels such as “View Property,” “Compare Projects,” and “Book a Consultation,” not vague “Discover More.”

Primary CTAs use squared, tailored proportions: 18–24 px horizontal padding, a 2 px radius, and a small arrow where it clarifies direction. An oxblood fill may be used for one high-priority editorial or campaign CTA on a parchment field; the website default remains Midnight Navy.

## 10. Photography, film, and 3D direction

### Image priorities

1. Property and architectural detail.
2. The view and surrounding neighbourhood.
3. Human moments that clarify scale and lifestyle.
4. Advisors in real settings.
5. Dubai context.

### Art direction

- Natural blue-hour, early-morning, and warm-evening light.
- True material colour with restrained grading.
- Calm camera movement and long, composed shots.
- International clients represented naturally across age, ethnicity, and family structure.
- Use skyline imagery only when it locates the property or story.
- Advisor portraits should feel like modern editorial portraiture: direct but natural gaze, tailored clothing, real architectural settings, soft directional light, and no staged “luxury agent” poses.
- Include crafted close-ups—stone edges, joinery, brass, books, tailoring, hands reviewing plans—to connect material quality with advisory care.
- British inspiration should be felt through composition and styling, never through London props placed in Dubai scenes.

### Homepage hero

Preferred: a 12–18 second muted cinematic loop with 4–5 shots—arrival, architectural reveal, interior detail, view, human scale. Provide a strong poster image and responsive crops.

If using an immersive 3D scene, it must serve a clear interaction such as exploring a residence, view, or neighbourhood. It should not delay the headline or CTA, and must fall back gracefully on mobile and low-power devices.

### Technical requirements

- Art-directed desktop and mobile crops, not one image forced into every ratio.
- WebP/AVIF images with explicit width and height.
- Video poster, captions where speech exists, pause control, and reduced-motion fallback.
- Do not autoplay audio.
- Performance target: the hero must not prevent good Core Web Vitals.

### AI-generated imagery

Permitted for mood exploration, concept visualisation, abstract transitions, and campaign artwork. Property marketing must not use generated images in a way that could be mistaken for the actual unit, view, amenity, or completion status. Label illustrative material clearly.

8. **Market intelligence** — latest report, chart, or advisor briefing.
9. **Meet the advisors** — named people, languages, and expertise.
10. **DARIE** — optional faster discovery module.
11. **Proof and trust** — verified testimonials, developer relationships, RERA details.
12. **Final consultation CTA** — concise form plus WhatsApp/phone alternative.

### Homepage visual rhythm

The homepage should not alternate identical card grids. Use this deliberate cadence:

1. **Cinematic** — dark, immersive hero with minimal copy.
2. **Editorial** — parchment field, serif statement, curated properties.
3. **Evidence** — structured facts and restrained rules.
4. **Human** — advisor or client-led story.
5. **Place** — full-bleed neighbourhood or residence film.
6. **Journal** — insight/report treatment with oxblood and folio detail.
7. **Service** — calm international-buyer pathway and consultation.

Limit prominent dark sections to roughly one-third of the homepage. The site must not become an uninterrupted navy canvas.

### Property card

Show only the information needed to choose the next click:

- Strong image or film indicator.
- Property/project name and location.
- Starting price or full price, correctly qualified.
- Beds/type and completion/status where relevant.
- One meaningful tag: “Waterfront,” “Ready,” “Q4 2028,” etc.
- Save and compare actions.

Do not cover photography with multiple badges, gradients, developer logos, and promotional ribbons.

### Property/project detail page

- Visual opening + essential facts.
- Clear enquiry and consultation actions.
- Story and differentiating features.
- Gallery/film.
- Location and travel context.
- Unit types and availability date.
- Payment plan and fees, with qualifications.
- Developer information and track record.
- Investment context with sources and risk-aware language.
- Downloads and floor plans.
- Named advisor.
- Related alternatives.

### International buyer journey

Provide dedicated content for process, documents, financing, reservation, conveyancing, fees, remote purchase, handover, property management, and residency-related questions. Use reviewed legal/financial wording and dates. This is a major trust and SEO asset, not a footer FAQ.

Create a reusable component library containing:

- Header: transparent, solid, menu-open, scrolled, and mobile variants.
- Buttons: default, hover, focus, pressed, loading, disabled.
- Inputs: default, focus, completed, error, disabled.
- Property cards: ready, off-plan, sold/unavailable, video, saved, compared.
- Filters: desktop bar, mobile drawer, empty state, applied state.
- Media: image, gallery, video, 3D, floor plan.
- Advisor card and contact panel.
- Testimonial with verified attribution.
- Stat with source/date.
- Chart, map, table, and disclosure.
- Modal/drawer, toast, breadcrumb, pagination, skeleton, empty state, and error state.
- Cookie/consent and lead-form consent patterns.

Every interactive element needs a visible keyboard focus state. Hover cannot reveal essential information unavailable on touch devices.

## 13. Motion system

Motion should feel architectural and cinematic: slow for atmosphere, quick for interface feedback.

| Motion | Duration | Behaviour |
|---|---:|---|
| Button/link feedback | 120–180 ms | Colour/underline/2–4 px arrow shift |
| Card/media hover | 240–360 ms | Subtle crop or scale, max 1.02 |
| Section reveal | 450–700 ms | Opacity + 12–24 px translation |
| Page transition | 350–600 ms | Simple fade/field transition |
| Cinematic ambient loop | 12–18 s | Calm edit; no rapid cuts |

- Use one easing family consistently.
- Do not animate every heading or number.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and replace parallax/3D/auto-advancing effects with stable alternatives.
- No custom cursor unless it adds a clear interaction benefit and remains accessible.

### Signature transitions

- Fine rules may draw from left to right as a section enters, once per section and within 400–600 ms.
- Editorial images may reveal through a restrained vertical mask; never mimic opening curtains or turning antique pages literally.
- The monogram may fade on film titles but must not spin, assemble, shine, or receive a metallic glint.
- Oxblood active-state changes should be immediate and functional, not decorative pulses.

## 14. DARIE: supporting product expression

DARIE should help a visitor narrow options quickly, then make the transition to a human advisor obvious.

### Visual treatment

- Lockwood & Carter identity remains dominant.
- Use signal blue only for listening, processing, and active voice states.
- A simple waveform or voice level can be used; avoid humanoid avatars and glowing sci-fi effects.
- Use calm prompts: “Tell us what you are looking for in Dubai.”

### UX requirements

- Provide text input as an equal alternative to voice.
- Explain what data is recorded or retained before microphone permission.
- Never present generated investment, legal, eligibility, or completion statements as guaranteed facts.
- Show sources or hand off to an advisor for material claims.
- Provide edit, restart, and human-contact paths at all times.

## 15. Market intelligence and data visualisation

- One primary message per chart.
- Use direct labels instead of legends where possible.
- Use 1.5–2 px lines, restrained gridlines, and no 3D or decorative gradients.
- Always show metric definition, geography, time period, update date, and source.
- Distinguish percentage change from percentage-point change.
- Do not mix asking prices, transaction prices, rents, and forecasts without explicit labelling.
- Provide a data table or accessible text summary for important charts.
- Forecasts must be labelled as forecasts and accompanied by assumptions or methodology.

Recommended report-cover formula:

**Market/date eyebrow → decisive finding → one hero number or location crop → source line → L&C wordmark**

Canva is appropriate for day-to-day marketing production after the master system is created. Start from British editorial, architecture journal, refined annual-report, book-cover, investment-report, and minimalist property layouts—not templates dominated by black marble, gold frames, faux heraldry, newspaper cosplay, agents pointing at text, or multiple badges.

- Property image remains the dominant element.
- Maximum two font families and three text sizes per frame.
- Keep logo placement and safe margins consistent.
- Put pricing/status qualifications on the same frame as the claim.
- Do not make every post navy; alternate parchment editorial, full-bleed property, and midnight data frames.
- Use oxblood as a campaign-series marker, folio, rule, or report field—not on every post.
- Keep brass flat and subtle in digital work; reserve foil simulation for clearly labelled print mock-ups.

| **The Journal** | Parchment, serif headline, folio, fine oxblood rule, disciplined image crop | Insights, neighbourhoods, advisor views |
| **The Residence** | Full-bleed property image, minimal midnight panel, small descriptor, quiet wordmark | Listings, launches, films |
| **The Briefing** | Midnight or white field, Manrope data, one brass rule, sourced chart | Market findings, reports, investor education |

All Canva masters must lock the logo safe area, type hierarchy, colour styles, disclaimers, and grid. Editable zones should be limited to image, approved text fields, date, price/status qualification, and CTA.

## 17. Open-design workflow

Use an open, structured design platform for the website system and Canva for controlled marketing output.

### Recommended stack

1. **Penpot** — source of truth for website layouts, responsive frames, tokens, reusable components, and prototypes. Its SVG/CSS-oriented workflow is suitable for developer handoff and can be self-hosted.
2. **Canva** — approved campaign and social templates for non-designers.
3. **SVG master assets** — logos and icons remain platform-independent.
4. **Design-token file** — colours, type, spacing, radii, and motion values stored in a code-friendly format and mapped to the website.

## 18. Accessibility, performance, and trust

Premium design must also feel effortless and credible.

### Accessibility

- WCAG 2.2 AA target.
- Keyboard-accessible navigation, galleries, filters, forms, modals, and maps.
- Visible focus styles.
- Semantic headings and meaningful link labels.
- Alternative text for meaningful images; empty alt text for decoration.
- Captions/transcripts for spoken media.
- Zoom and reflow up to 200% without loss of content or function.
- RTL-ready layouts if Arabic is launched.

### Performance

- Mobile-first loading strategy.
- Avoid loading 3D, maps, and full video before they are needed.
- Set image dimensions, responsive sources, and lazy loading below the fold.
- Self-host or efficiently subset fonts.
- Measure Core Web Vitals on real mid-range mobile devices and UAE/international networks.

### Trust and compliance

- Display current brokerage/RERA details in the footer and relevant enquiry/property contexts after verification.
- Date all market data and buying-process guidance.
- Clearly label illustrative renders, starting prices, availability, payment plans, and expected completion dates.
- Add consent wording for phone, email, WhatsApp, and microphone use where applicable.
- Do not use developer logos without permission or imply an exclusive relationship without evidence.

## 19. Quality-control checklist

Before a page or campaign is approved:

- Does the first screen clearly say Dubai real estate and what the visitor can do next?
- Is brokerage/advisory more prominent than AI?
- Is the message supported by current, verifiable proof?
- Is there one dominant visual idea rather than several competing effects?
- Are the typography, colour, spacing, and shape tokens followed?
- Does it work at 360 px, 768 px, 1280 px, and 1600 px?
- Are keyboard, focus, contrast, reduced-motion, and error states designed?
- Are price, availability, date, source, and render qualifications present?
- Is there a direct route to a named advisor?
- Does the page remain useful if video, 3D, map, or DARIE fails to load?
- Does the work feel British-inspired through restraint and craft rather than flags, crests, or invented history?
- Are Baskerville, Manrope, parchment, midnight, oxblood, and brass used according to their defined roles?
- Is there enough Dubai light, architecture, and local specificity to prevent the brand from feeling transplanted from London?
- Would the design still look premium in one colour and without animation or metallic finish?

## 20. Delivery roadmap

### Phase 1 — strategic foundation

- Confirm proposition, audiences, services, proof, and approved claims.
- Approve the visual concept and typography on a single brand board.
- Approve three modern-heritage logo routes—editorial wordmark, framed monogram, and restrained heritage lockup—then commission one route; do not let it block initial wireframes.
- Define measurement goals: qualified enquiries, consultation bookings, saves, comparisons, and report downloads.

### Phase 2 — UX and design system

- Create sitemap and international-buyer journey.
- Build tokens, core components, and all required states.
- Prototype homepage, search, and property/project detail in desktop and mobile.
- User-test with UAE-based and overseas buyers.

### Phase 3 — content and media

- Commission hero film/poster, flagship property stories, advisor portraits, and neighbourhood imagery.
- Create verified proof library and data-source register.
- Write buying guides and market-report templates.

### Phase 4 — build and validation

- Implement responsive components and CMS fields.
- Add analytics and consent-aware lead attribution.
- Complete accessibility, performance, browser, form, and content QA.
- Soft launch; review behaviour and enquiry quality before adding more visual effects.

### Phase 5 — campaign system

- Translate approved components into Canva master templates.
- Train the marketing team on safe editing zones and claim qualification.
- Review the template library quarterly and archive inconsistent variants.
